# AI-assisted revision disclosure and reviewer-response protocol

Use this reference when a reviewer questions whether a manuscript, revision, or response was generated by AI.

## Immediate response discipline

1. Freeze uploads and reviewer replies. Do not answer an accusation about AI-generated prose with another polished response produced in minutes.
2. Separate the reviewer’s tone from the verifiable claims. Do not speculate that the reviewer is jealous, hostile, or acting in bad faith.
3. Audit each concrete criticism against the exact uploaded PDF and raw artifacts—not a newer local draft.
4. Distinguish training loss, evaluation NLL, rolling/EMA smoothing, and last-step values. If batch-level provenance or the evaluator definition is missing, remove or narrow the numeric claim rather than arguing from a summary CSV.
5. Treat mixed train/eval prose and tables as a presentation defect even when the underlying numbers are individually correct.

## Honest disclosure structure

Describe contributions by function:

- Human author: research question, scientific mechanism, experimental design, run/stop decisions, interpretation, and final responsibility.
- AI-assisted tools: drafting, language editing, restructuring, implementation assistance, artifact parsing, or diagnostics—only the uses that actually occurred.

Avoid both extremes:

- Do not deny substantial assistance when it occurred.
- Do not say “the paper was delegated to AI” when the author directed the work, supplied evidence, made decisions, and retained responsibility; that phrasing implies abandonment of control.
- Do not claim that every output was verified if the rapid revision demonstrably introduced inconsistencies. Acknowledge insufficient verification and accept responsibility.
- Do not attribute autonomous invention of the project or method to AI when that is factually false, but do not use this point to obscure substantial drafting or coding assistance.

A defensible structure is:

1. acknowledge why the speed raised concern;
2. state the human scientific contributions concretely;
3. disclose the specific AI-assisted tasks;
4. acknowledge any insufficiently checked rapid revision;
5. state that no further rapid revision will be submitted before a manual evidence audit.

The final message should be rewritten by the author in their own voice. The assistant may fact-check it, identify unsupported denials, and check consistency, but should not create another response that masquerades as unaided human prose.

## Withdrawal guard

OpenReview “Withdrawal” normally concerns the entire submission, not merely one revision or response. Before acting, confirm explicitly whether the user intends to:

- withdraw the complete submission;
- remove or supersede only a recent revision/comment, if the venue permits it; or
- leave the record unchanged pending the Action Editor.

Do not execute full withdrawal from an emotional or ambiguous instruction such as “delete this.” Preserve the distinction between a reviewer recommendation and an editorial decision.

## Resubmission recovery path

When the interaction is compromised, prefer an offline evidence rebuild over repeated rebuttal:

- trace every headline metric to raw evaluator outputs;
- use a genuinely held-out evaluation stream;
- separate train and evaluation tables;
- match tokenizer, data order, budget, seed policy, schedule, and hardware where the claim requires matching;
- run the named learned/fixed/dense controls at the claimed budget;
- remove audit-log prose and replace it with normal scientific descriptions;
- let the author perform a slow, sentence-level final read before upload.
